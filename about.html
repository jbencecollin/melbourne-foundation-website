Our story

About The Foundation

In 2003, four friends who met while studying at university decided they would actively try to make a difference in the community after they graduated.

What we do

Transformational opportunities for talented, driven students

They wanted to make the largest difference possible in society and believed that education would provide the best avenue to open opportunities in a child's life. From this concept, The Melbourne Foundation was founded in 2006.

Through our scholarship program, The Melbourne Foundation has fostered the intellectual and cultural development of Australian school children, providing transformational opportunities to talented, driven, yet financially disadvantaged students.

The Melbourne Foundation offers scholarships to male and female students for entry into year 9 at private schools in the Melbourne metropolitan area.

How scholarships are funded

Funded by a community of donors

The Melbourne Foundation's scholarships were initially funded by its founders. In August 2012, The Melbourne Foundation was endorsed as a Deductible Gift Recipient and started receiving external donations. Now, The Melbourne Foundation's scholarships are primarily funded through contributions from:

  • The management of the Foundation
  • Syndicates of individuals who come together to sponsor a scholarshipFor example, syndicates may be formed by parents of current students at a school, ex-students of a school, or like-minded friends.
  • Philanthropic organisations
  • Other members of the public

Foundation Patron

PS

Paul Sheahan

Foundation Patron

Paul Sheahan spent his professional career in education, first teaching Mathematics at a number of independent schools in Victoria and the United Kingdom, before taking on the responsibility of heading two schools: The Geelong College for nine-and-a-half years and then Melbourne Grammar School for fourteen years. In addition, he played Test cricket for Australia in a career spanning the late-1960s and early-1970s.

Since retirement from the education scene, he has been involved with a number of other community activities, including board positions on not-for-profit institutions. He is also President of the Melbourne Cricket Club, the club entrusted with the management of that sporting icon, the Melbourne Cricket Ground.

Paul is particularly keen to facilitate ways for people who have had a relatively privileged upbringing to in turn provide similar opportunities for others from financially disadvantaged backgrounds.
